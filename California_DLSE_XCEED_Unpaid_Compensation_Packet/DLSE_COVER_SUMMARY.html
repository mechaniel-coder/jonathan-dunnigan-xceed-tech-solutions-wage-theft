7. X-CEED's Denial and Why It Fails

X-CEED sent a formal denial letter on May 30, 2026 (from Jonathan Dunnigan, jzdunnigan@yahoo.com). The letter:

Why each argument fails:

"No signed employment agreement"

This does not mean $0 is owed. Even without a signed agreement, payment is owed under theories of requested services, accepted services, benefit received, quantum meruit, unjust enrichment, and implied contract. X-CEED cannot request and benefit from work and then pay nothing by saying the final employment draft was unsigned.

Section XV argument is self-defeating

X-CEED quoted Section XV of the same document it calls void, claiming Discord messages are "invalidated." But the actual text of Section XV states:

"No verbal statement, silence, delay, continued work, staff title, Discord role, or informal message shall waive Employee's right to compensation, commissions, deferred compensation, hardship reserve balances, or equity unless the waiver is in a separate written agreement signed by Employee."

This clause protects Daniel's right to compensation. It prevents X-CEED from claiming Daniel waived his pay by continuing to work. X-CEED misread their own quoted clause — it helps Daniel, not X-CEED. Discord messages remain fully usable as evidence.

Section XIII makes Discord messages explicitly admissible as evidence

Section XIII of the same agreement states:

"The Parties agree that written communications, including emails, Discord messages, payroll records, invoices, ledgers, screenshots, tickets, project-management records, commits, and signed electronic documents may be used as evidence of work assignments, compensation terms, approvals, notices, equity commitments, payment requests, and disputes."

X-CEED cannot argue that Discord messages are invalid evidence while simultaneously being bound by a term in their own employment agreement that explicitly makes Discord messages admissible as evidence of compensation terms and payment disputes. This is a direct, express contractual provision. X-CEED drafted and sent this agreement.

Section XX makes Discord acknowledgments binding on X-CEED

Section XX (Electronic Signatures) states:

"electronic signatures, typed names, scanned signatures, verified e-signature platforms, email confirmations, and Discord acknowledgments may be used to show acceptance of this Agreement... A signature or written confirmation from an authorized Employer representative shall bind Employer to this Agreement if the representative has authority to bind X-CEED TECH SOLUTIONS LLC."

This means any Discord message from Jonathan Dunnigan confirming CTO role, compensation, payroll, or terms constitutes binding employer acceptance of those terms.

Internal inconsistency

X-CEED cannot claim the unsigned draft has "no legal authority" for compensation purposes while simultaneously invoking a clause from that same draft to try to eliminate Daniel's evidence. The argument is internally contradictory.

Double-billing accusation

X-CEED's double-billing accusation is unsubstantiated and is directly refuted by the documentary record.

Chronological separation: Daniel began work for X-CEED on May 19, 2026. The separate Xerox/XBUniverse contractor agreement was not signed until May 25, 2026 at 2:38 PM — six days into the X-CEED work period. This means Days 1 through 6 of the X-CEED engagement (May 19–24) were entirely outside any Xerox relationship.

Complete project separation: The corrected Xerox timesheet expressly states: "No billable hours are claimed prior to contract execution." It covers only May 25 (post 2:38 PM) through May 28. The X-CEED deliverables (Discord hosting bot with Stripe/OAuth/Pterodactyl integration, IP registration portal, Docker infrastructure across 13 containers, iptables hardening, DNS/domain configuration) are categorically and completely different from the Xerox deliverables (Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod in C#/C++, Redscript, XAML/WPF launcher, native DLL, live game test). No deliverable, invoice line, or false statement has been identified that constitutes double-billing.

X-CEED has not identified: the specific date, time block, service, amount, or false statement it claims was billed twice. Without a specific identification, there is no valid double-billing claim under any legal standard.

Downloads corroborate separation: The full-period timesheet shows X-CEED LLC filing PDFs downloaded May 21; IRS EIN confirmation (CP_575_A.pdf) downloaded May 21, 1:47 PM; employment agreement draft downloaded May 22, 4:30 PM. Daniel was reviewing X-CEED's own company formation and employment documents during CTO onboarding — prior to any Xerox involvement.

Shifting explanations
